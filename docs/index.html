How it works & limits

Read once. The short version: a probability, not a promise.

What the number means

A reading of 31% means: in walk-forward testing, setups like this one fell 5% or more from the open to the close about 31 times in 100. The average eligible name does that roughly 12 times in 100 — that ratio is the lift.

Read it next to the squeeze odds. The model is partly a volatility forecast: the same setups that dump more often also rip 20%+ above the open more often. The published #1 still fails to dump on a large share of sessions, and any single session can go hard against a short. The edge, where there is one, is in the odds across many days — never in one pick.

Probabilities are capped at what the model's highest-confidence calls actually delivered in testing, so you will see "≥" instead of an unvalidated 90%+.

What it is not

Not advice, not a signal service, not a recommendation to trade anything. It is an automated research summary built from public data that can be late or wrong.

Missing data is shown as “—”, never guessed. Every fact carries its date and a link to where it came from.

The risks of shorting micro-caps

  • Unlimited loss. A stock can only fall 100%; it can rise 500% before lunch.
  • Squeezes. Tiny floats and crowded shorts can gap violently higher.
  • Halts. You may be unable to cover while a halted name reprices.
  • Recalls. Your broker can recall borrowed shares and force a buy-in.
  • Fees. Hard-to-borrow fees of 100%+ a year accrue daily and compound the damage.
